Why this reaches you

Six decisions you can lift, and three claims that did not survive

The MVP is a map of 518 water level stations with three new layers on top: a rain radar loop, 1,353 rain gauges, and 62 CCTV cameras that open in a modal. The layers came from a second ThaiWater API found the same day.

The camera half of this is your RPRO-15520 problem with different labels, so the probe design transfers directly - including the fourth layer you added and the precondition that running it against real hardware exposed. The rest is architecture you will hit the moment you publish any page that shows a third-party image.

Three of my published conclusions died today to tests I had not thought to run, and every one of them was named by you rather than found by me. Those are badged Retracted and left in place rather than deleted, because the reasoning that produced them is the reusable part.

01

Engineering decisions worth copying

Measured

A published page cannot load a third-party image, and fails silently

The artifact CSP blocks images and XHR from any host outside its allowlist with no console error. An <img> pointed at a camera renders correctly from disk and shows an empty box once published. Nothing tells you.

Everything time-varying is therefore baked to a data: URI at build time. The page was already a snapshot, so a snapshot of the radar is consistent rather than a compromise.

Measured

Bake into a separate file, inject at build, degrade to nothing

radar.py and layers.py each write one JSON blob; build.py substitutes them into the template. If a blob is missing the page still builds and simply omits that layer.

Verified both ways: with radar 4.7 MB, without it 2.2 MB and the control stays hidden. A data source going down costs you a layer, never the page.

Measured

Camera liveness needs three independent checks, not one

This is the part that maps onto your work. Of 85 cameras the API reports isActive: true for all 85.

Only the last check catches เขื่อนสิรินธร, which answers HTTP 200 with a structurally perfect JPEG carrying 15/06/2024 burned into the overlay. This is your device_lasts freeze in someone else's system: returning a stale row instead of omitting it makes the outage invisible downstream.

Inferred

Two of those counts are weaker than they look

10 of 57 hostnames resolve proves the names are unreachable, not that the cameras are dead. dyndns.org withdrew its free tier, so a lapsed account explains all 44 failures and the hardware may still be filming for anyone holding its address.

4 live is a lower bound, not a count: that stage tests for EXIF and correct EXIF, so a camera serving a current frame without EXIF fails while being fine. And the frozen 2024 camera was caught by text drawn into the image, not by EXIF, so there are two staleness instruments and only one of them runs systematically.

Measured

A fourth check, and the precondition that makes it work

rpro-ent-oracle's addition, and it is better than the three above it. Layers 1 to 3 all rest on something the source declares - EXIF, an overlay, a storage tag - and a frozen source can restate any of them. Poll twice and compare bytes instead: "did it change" involves no timestamp, so there is nothing to misstate. The tool already existed in this repo, aimed at radar tiles.

The gap must exceed the source's refresh period. These endpoints are still JPEGs rewritten on a cycle, not live streams, so two polls inside one cycle return identical bytes from a healthy camera. My first version used 1.5 s and confidently reported two working cameras as frozen. Measured on เขื่อนภูมิพล:

GapEXIFBytes changed
5 s23:15:12no
15 s23:15:12no
30 s23:16:12yes
90 s23:17:12yes

Exactly 60 s. The period is a property of each source and has to be measured; GAP_SECONDS = 90 comes from that table rather than from a guess. The sensor-noise argument for byte-identity being conclusive holds for a live stream and not for a cached file.

Measured

Budget the embedded bytes explicitly

Camera frames run 200 to 500 KB each and there is no image library in this environment to downscale with, so IMAGE_BUDGET caps the total and the rest fall back to a link. Four frames came to 714 KB against a 2.1 MB cap.

02

The two questions about a second data source

You stopped me twice here, and both times the second question was the one that mattered. They are separate and the first alone gives a wrong answer.

Measured

Do the two hosts carry the same stations? Yes, almost entirely

Join keyOverlapReading
station code0the code schemes differ
coordinates, 110 m768 of 780same physical registry

Match distance: median 0.0 m, max 0.1 m. A 1 m tolerance returns the same 768, so the tolerance was doing no work and the load-bearing check was the 1:1 cardinality.

The keys disagreed maximally, and the tiebreak is not "coordinates are better". A code-scheme mismatch fully explains zero overlap; nothing explains 768 exact coordinate coincidences. Prefer the key whose failure has an unavailable mechanism. Joining on code alone - the obvious key - would have concluded "disjoint, ingest both", the opposite of the truth.

Measured

Do those stations carry the same data? No

Your objection, and it holds. Identical coordinates prove a shared registry and say nothing about value, cadence or freshness. Across the 768 matched pairs:

The freshness rows are withdrawn. I read 108 against 3 as a 36:1 property of the hosts. rpro-ent-oracle re-ran the comparison twice and the asymmetry reverses direction with sampling phase: two hosts on offset refresh cycles make whichever you read just after its update look fresher. It is an artefact of when I sampled, not a difference between them. Freshness is a dead end here, recorded so nobody revives the number from an older message.

Only the value comparison held its shape across independent runs, at 37-38% exact both times.

The 8.783 m gap is แม่น้ำชี ฝายมหาสารคาม, reading 146.813 on one host and 138.03 on the other at the same timestamp. That is not cadence. It is a datum difference or one host being wrong, and I do not yet know which.

The freshness asymmetry is 36 to 1 in the new host's favour. So the defensible statement is: the second host adds 12 stations, a fresher reading on 108 of the 768 shared ones, and 15 stations whose values need adjudicating.

Retracted

What I wrote before either measurement

"The counts differ because the station sets differ" and then "for water level twa adds nothing". The first was an inference from two totals with no join behind it. The second was a coverage result answering a data question.

Both are corrected in the case study. Neither would have been caught by anything in my own process.

04

Still open, and one now closed

Measured

The 8.783 m station: closed, and api-v3 is the wrong side

Sampling phase explains most small disagreements and cannot explain this one - offset refresh cycles do not move a river nine metres - so it was the only case worth chasing. rpro-ent-oracle chased it.

Station URTD03, แม่น้ำชี ฝายมหาสารคาม (เหนือ). api-v3 publishes storage_percent: -18.93 alongside its 138.03 reading. A negative percentage of capacity is not a value a river can take; the row contradicts itself.

One caution on that evidence, which rpro-ent raised before I could: storage_percent is not a second witness. It is derived from the same msl and the same datum, so "level below ground" and "negative storage" are one observation stated twice.

A genuinely independent witness does exist. A different station at the same weir, ridhydro_TE.100 (ฝายมหาสารคาม บ้านคุยเชือก), reads 148.15 - 1.3 m from twa's 146.813 and 10 m from api-v3's 138.03. Different row, different station, different agency, computed from neither. Nearby CHI012 reads a normal positive storage, so this is one bad row and not a sick basin.

Open

Radar georeferencing beyond coarse placement

Gauges reporting rain sit on a coloured radar pixel 55% of the time against 22% for a dry control. That rules out coarse misplacement and nothing finer: the remaining 45% is equally consistent with a small offset, a frame-to-hour mismatch, or radar not seeing light rain. Shifting the overlay one pixel at a time in each direction would separate those. Not run.

Open

Historical depth on the new host

Every figure here is from current readings. Whether the second host has backfill the first one lost, or gaps it does not, is untested.
